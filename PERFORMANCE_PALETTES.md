# MCFA Performance Palettes

This file holds reusable creative preferences for live and scheduled MCFA
performances. It is intentionally about perception and musical behavior, not CLI
flags or lane programming. Future Codex sessions should use the rapid intake
below, save the user's answers under **Saved palettes**, and treat each palette
as a possibility space rather than a score.

## How to ask

Ask the questions in quick, readable batches. Keep each question to one line and
offer short options. The user may answer with words, numbers, ranges, several
choices, `either`, or `skip`. Follow up only when an answer is internally
contradictory or would materially change the experience. Never require the user
to answer every item.

For scheduled performances, collect several acceptable answers where possible.
On each run, select a distinct combination, then keep conducting dynamically;
do not merely choose one palette and render it as a fixed arrangement. Preserve
explicit dislikes and safety constraints across every combination.

## Rapid creative intake

1. **Length:** brief, 1 minute, 2 minutes, extended, or a range?
2. **Speed:** glacial, slow, lurching, medium, fast, feral, or several speeds at once?
3. **Pulse:** obvious, half-hidden, fragmented, mostly free, or no shared pulse?
4. **Clock relationships:** synchronized, polymetric, unrelated, drifting, or mixed?
5. **Density:** skeletal, spacious, busy, saturated, or constantly breathing in and out?
6. **Turnover:** stable identities, gradual mutation, frequent replacement, or ruthless churn?
7. **Intervention rate:** occasional large changes, steady mutations, or near-constant disruption?
8. **Pitch versus noise:** mostly pitched, balanced, mostly noise, or changing over time?
9. **Root or tonal center:** a note, several competing centers, no center, or choose for me?
10. **Mode or pitch language:** choose one or several from the menu below.
11. **Mode strictness:** recognizable, loosely implied, frequently contaminated, or merely a starting point?
12. **Register:** subterranean, low-heavy, midrange pressure, brittle highs, full-spectrum, or violently colliding?
13. **Note shapes:** single tones, clusters, changing chords, wide intervals, crawling lines, or mixtures?
14. **Attacks:** blunt, percussive, splintered, scraping, blooming, smeared, or mixed?
15. **Material image:** wooden, metallic, glassy, rubbery, electrical, corroded, submerged, dusty, or another word?
16. **Distortion:** clean tension, frayed edges, driven, crushed, or varying by process?
17. **Filtering:** dark and buried, nasal, hollow, razor-bright, slowly moving, violently animated, or mixed?
18. **Space:** dry and close, small room, cavernous, dub-like echoes, unstable depth, or conflicting spaces?
19. **Stereo motion:** mostly fixed, slow drift, sharp jumps, orbiting, unstable, or mixed?
20. **Repetition:** hypnotic, subtly mutating, interrupted, constantly rewritten, or barely repetitive?
21. **Silence:** rare, puncturing gaps, long exposed holes, abrupt dropouts, or density without relief?
22. **Process independence:** a loose family, unrelated machines, active collisions, or near-total disagreement?
23. **Recognizable identities:** should named sounds remain traceable, mutate beyond recognition, or alternate between both?
24. **Rhythmic metaphor:** drill splinters, damaged machinery, ritual pulse, insect activity, collapsing architecture, weather, or another image?
25. **Emotional temperature:** cold, hostile, mournful, ecstatic, paranoid, playful, numb, feverish, alien, or mixed?
26. **Dramatic behavior:** non-narrative ecology, recurring states, sudden regime changes, slow drift, or an explicit arc?
27. **Ending:** deadline catches it mid-life, gradual evaporation, hard severing, sparse residue, or choose in the moment?
28. **Surprise allowance:** stay near the brief, take occasional outside turns, or actively betray expectations?
29. **Must include:** any sound, behavior, mode, register, or metaphor that needs to appear?
30. **Must avoid:** any genre habit, timbre, rhythm, emotional cliché, register, or ending you do not want?
31. **Compositional practice:** aleatoric cells, postmodern collage, process music, unstable minimalism, serial-like constraints, spectral clusters, or mixed?
32. **Beat vocabulary:** industrial machinery, death-industrial crawl, gabber pressure, otaku-speedcore splinters, synthetic chopped break, or no genre anchor?
33. **Tuplet pressure:** occasional triplet interruption, sustained triplet grid, binary-versus-ternary conflict, rapid tuplets, or mostly non-metric?
34. **Break behavior:** recognizable synthetic break contour, heavily chopped, repeatedly replaced, dissolved into noise, or only brief quotations?
35. **Musical perplexity:** very low, low, medium, high, extreme, or independently different by lane?
36. **Perplexity contour:** steady, slowly wandering, sudden switches, alternating clear/opaque states, breathing, or freely conducted?
37. **Entropy:** repeatable seeds, fresh system entropy, entropy on selected lanes, reseed replacements, or mixed?

## Mode and pitch-language menu

- **Ionian / major** (bright, settled, exposed)
- **Dorian** (cool minor, driving, quietly defiant)
- **Phrygian** (claustrophobic, hostile, ritualistic)
- **Lydian** (weightless, luminous, uncanny)
- **Mixolydian** (open, swaggering, unresolved)
- **Aeolian / natural minor** (dark, mournful, familiar)
- **Locrian** (unstable, ruined, unable to rest)
- **Harmonic minor** (scorched, theatrical, sharply tense)
- **Melodic minor** (slippery, sophisticated, unsettled)
- **Pentatonic** (spare, elemental, open)
- **Whole-tone** (floating, dreamlike, directionless)
- **Diminished / octatonic** (mechanical, anxious, coiled)
- **Chromatic** (cramped, abrasive, compulsive)
- **Atonal / free pitch** (untethered, confrontational, alien)
- **Detuned tonal** (familiar pitch seen through melting glass)
- **Several competing modes** (multiple emotional climates colliding)

## Compositional-practice menu

- **Aleatoric cells** (chance-directed events inside clear local constraints)
- **Postmodern collage** (incompatible historical and popular languages sharing one space)
- **Process music** (a rule audibly transforming itself over time)
- **Unstable minimalism** (repetition whose ground keeps slipping)
- **Serial-like constraints** (ordered pitch or rhythm cells without academic imitation)
- **Spectral clusters** (timbre and beating relationships treated as harmonic material)
- **Indeterminate form** (entry, exit, density, and duration decided during performance)

## Beat-language menu

- **Industrial machinery** (repetitive metal pressure, impacts, friction, failing motors)
- **Death-industrial crawl** (slow oppressive mass, contaminated drones, blunt ritual hits)
- **Gabber pressure** (fast distorted-feeling low attacks, hard grids, violent interruption)
- **Otaku-speedcore splinters** (hyperactive bright fragments, severe cuts, extreme subdivisions)
- **Triplet machinery** (ternary propulsion, rolling impacts, binary-versus-triplet conflict)
- **Synthetic chopped break** (Amen-like syncopation built from synthesized impacts, never a sample)
- **Break disintegration** (a recognizable contour repeatedly chopped, replaced, and dissolved)

## Musical-perplexity menu

Perplexity describes how confidently a listener can predict what happens next.
It is independent of speed, density, loudness, dissonance, and genre.

- **Very low** (ritual, transparent, nearly inevitable)
- **Low** (readable patterns with small deviations)
- **Medium** (recognizable rules repeatedly questioned)
- **High** (volatile relationships and a short predictive horizon)
- **Extreme** (opaque, discontinuous, almost impossible to model locally)
- **Split by lane** (some machines remain obvious while others resist prediction)

Perplexity may also move through time:

- **Steady** (one consistent degree of predictability)
- **Slow wandering** (gradual drift between clarity and uncertainty)
- **Sudden switches** (the governing logic changes without warning)
- **Alternating clear/opaque** (legible islands interrupted by unreadable states)
- **Breathing** (perplexity expands and contracts without a song-like arc)
- **Freely conducted** (Codex decides from the current measured state)

## Compact answer example

The user never needs to copy this exact format; it only demonstrates how short
the answers can be:

> 2 minutes; several speeds; fragmented pulse; unrelated clocks; breathing
> density; ruthless churn; balanced pitch/noise; C and F# competing; Phrygian +
> whole-tone, contaminated; full-spectrum collisions; clusters and crawling
> lines; metallic/electrical; unstable depth; puncturing gaps; non-narrative;
> deadline catches it alive; avoid four-on-the-floor and tidy finales.

## Saved palettes

### 2026-08-14 — Expanded composition and beat vocabulary

This is an approved possibility pool, not a requirement to use every element in
every set:

- Draw from postmodern-classical composition, aleatoric practice, industrial,
  death industrial, gabber, and otaku-speedcore behavior.
- Triplet and binary-versus-ternary structures are welcome.
- Amen-like chopped-break behavior is welcome, but it must be described and
  implemented as synthetic because MCFA has no sample playback.
- Use these languages to increase representational diversity across scheduled
  performances. Recombine them differently and continue live mutation rather
  than turning the list into a fixed crossover arrangement.

When the user completes a full intake, append additional dated, plainly named
palettes here. Preserve their own words where possible, distinguish firm
constraints from optional choices, and allow multiple palettes to coexist so
scheduled performances can vary substantially.
